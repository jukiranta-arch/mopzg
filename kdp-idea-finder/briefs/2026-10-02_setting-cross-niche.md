# Setting research across niches (2026-10-02)

Juho's question: which settings sell in other niches that buyers of murder-mystery puzzle books also buy from, but haven't been used for a name-list murder puzzle yet?

**Data:** amazon.com Best Sellers lists, captured 2026-10-01/02, 2 pages each (about 100 books per list):
- Cozy Mystery
- Historical Mystery
- Traditional Detectives
- Coloring Books for Grown-Ups

Settings were read from titles and series names, then compared with the ~5,800 books already captured. This is one day's lists, and October lifts the Halloween and autumn books.

## What sells in the neighbouring niches

**Cozy mystery fiction**
- **Retired amateur sleuths in a club:**
  - Richard Osman's *Thursday Murder Club* books hold #1 and six more places across the lists;
  - also *The Marlow Murder Club*, *Golden Girls Cozy Mystery*, *The Oxford Detective Society*, *The Coffee Shop Detectives*, *Cooking Club Murder Mystery*, *Homefront Sleuths* (4 titles).
- **The English village or countryside, sold to US readers as "cozy English murder mystery":**
  - *Murder Most Bookish*, *A Country Meadow Murder*, *The English Village Murder*, *Murder in the Vineyard*, *Murder at the Manor House*;
  - Louise Penny's Three Pines (a Canadian village, 9 titles);
  - Miss Marple.
- **Witches and paranormal cozy:** *Wicked Witches of Cauldron Hollow* (3 titles), *Nightshade Detective Agency*, *Spell's Angels*, *Witchcakes*, *Lavender Lake*, *Havenwood*.
- **Bakeries and cafés:** *Bakeshop*, *Beacon Bakeshop*, *Coffeehouse*, *Peridale Cafe*, *Murder by Cheesecake*.

**Historical mystery**
- **Victorian London:** Magda Alexander (6 titles), Wrexford & Sloane, Anne Perry, Sherlock Holmes.
- **Regency and the 1920s–30s:** Ginger Gold, Lady Hardcastle, *The Detection Club*.
- **WWII home front:** *Homefront Sleuths*, *The Blackout Murders*.

**Adult coloring**
- **"Cozy" and "spooky-cute":**
  - #1 *Spooky Cutie*;
  - *Cozy Girl Season*, *Little Spooky*, *Spooky Cozy*, *Cozy Friends*, *Little Corner (Cozy Spaces)*, *Spooky Places*, *Gilmore Ghouls*, *Cutest Serial Killers*, *Fuzzy Hygge*.
- **Gardens and flowers.**

**Read:** the biggest gap with the strongest demand is **a club of retired amateur sleuths**:
- it is the top cozy-fiction trend (Osman, *Marlow*, *Golden Girls*), boosted by TV and film adaptations;
- no name-list puzzle book uses it;
- its readers are the same buyers as ours;
- it also fits the linked-case structure, with the same club taking on five cases and recurring characters for a series.

The historical periods (Victorian, 1920s, WWII home front) are the next gap. Each has only one or two puzzle attempts, and those are large print or unranked.

**Risks:**
- The club trend leans on one author and one TV and film moment, so it may fade.
- "Thursday Murder Club" is a brand: never in the title or keywords. "Murder club" itself is generic; *Marlow* and others use it.
