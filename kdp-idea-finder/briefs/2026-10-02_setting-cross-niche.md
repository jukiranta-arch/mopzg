# Setting research across niches (2026-10-02)

Juho's question: which settings sell in other niches that buyers of murder-mystery puzzle books also buy from, but haven't been used for a name-list murder puzzle yet?

**Data:** amazon.com Best Sellers lists, captured 2026-10-01/02, 2 pages each (about 100 books per list):
- Cozy Mystery
- Historical Mystery
- Traditional Detectives
- Coloring Books for Grown-Ups

Settings were read from titles and series names, then compared with the ~5,800 books already captured. This is one day's lists, and October lifts the Halloween and autumn books.

**Read:** the biggest gap with the strongest demand is **a club of retired amateur sleuths**:
- it is the top cozy-fiction trend (Osman, *Marlow*, *Golden Girls*), boosted by TV and film adaptations;
- no name-list puzzle book uses it;
- its readers are the same buyers as ours;
- it also fits the linked-case structure, with the same club taking on five cases and recurring characters for a series.

The historical periods (Victorian, 1920s, WWII home front) are the next gap. Each has only one or two puzzle attempts, and those are large print or unranked.

**Risks:**
- The club trend leans on one author and one TV and film moment, so it may fade.
- "Thursday Murder Club" is a brand: never in the title or keywords. "Murder club" itself is generic; *Marlow* and others use it.

**`victorian murder mystery puzzle book`.** Plenty of Victorian puzzle books, and none sells:

## Conclusion

In murder-mystery puzzle books, settings don't make winners. Every period and theme we tested has been tried, and the attempts sell little. The winners are first to a strong hook:
- *Alice*: the first of its kind;
- *Darcy*: famous names to spot;
- *Never Checked In*: records with details;
- *Kill Grid*: connected word-search cases;
- *Eliminate*: categories that lead to suspects.

**The setting's job** is to appeal to the buyer and not look like a copy. On that test, the cozy club of retired amateur sleuths (the #1 cozy-fiction trend) is the best fit: puzzle books use the word "club" only as branding, and none has a winner. The American small-town shops are second. The hotel, the ticket and the historical periods are crowded with failed attempts.
