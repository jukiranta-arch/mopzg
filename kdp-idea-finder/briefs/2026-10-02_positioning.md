**What the titles have in common:**
- a short hook phrase with "Killer" in it, or a "Who…?" question;

Buyers read that formula as "this is the cross-out-names game". We keep it.

## What is ours (each against a recorded complaint or gap)

1. **A story that pays off.**
   - Five murders, five killers, all sent by one person in town, over a 30-year-old secret.
   - "Just names, no story" is the lane's most repeated 1-star complaint.
   - The mastermind idea itself exists in word-search books (*The Mastermind Isn't Who You Think*, *The Black Seal Murders*), but not in a name-list book.
2. **Check every answer in the book, without spoilers.** No competitor does this. They use a website, a QR code, or the answer printed at the back.
3. **One evening per case** instead of one 46,000-name pile. "Too big" and "tedious" are recurring complaints.
4. **Cozy small-town America.**
   - Cozy sells in story-plus-word-search books (*Murder Among the Stacks* 30/day, *Murder for Breakfast* 15/day).
   - No name-list book uses it.
5. **The cut-paper silhouette art.** It is unlike every competitor's interior: those use AI realism, hatched ink, or none.

**The catalyst (the one line that makes a buyer stop):**

> **Five murders. Five killers. One person in town sent them all.**

It promises a story and a twist in seven words. It also answers the fear of a pointless name pile. The numbers line carries the genre signal.

**Keep the headline on one big case.** The "Find the Killer Mystery Files" series ("Five 555-Suspect Puzzles") sells 0.1/day per book. So the headline is one mastermind; "linked" says the five cases are one story.

## Title

**Decided (2026-10-02): *Who Sent the Killers?***
- It is the question the whole book answers.
- It uses the "Who…?" form of *Who Killed Mr Darcy?* and keeps "Killers" for search.
- No book with that title was found.

**The setting stays Juniper Falls,** inside the book only.
- **Why it's safe:** an amazon.com search for "juniper falls" (109 results) shows four romance and historical-fiction series by that name and one thriller. There is no puzzle book and no murder-mystery puzzle book.
- **What keeps it from clashing:**
  - the name stays out of the title, subtitle and keywords;
  - it is not the KDP series name (Amazon already has four "Juniper Falls" series pages).

**Subtitle** (title and subtitle together must be under 200 characters; this is 164):

> Who Sent the Killers?: A Find-the-Killer Murder Mystery Puzzle Book for Adults. 18,000 Suspects, 5 Linked Murders, 1 Mastermind. Check Every Answer Without Spoilers

**Series:** if volume two comes, the series name is about the puzzle format, not the town.

## Cover

Juho makes it in ChatGPT, in the same cut-paper silhouette style as the interior.

**Layout, top to bottom:**
1. **Title:** huge, in white or cream, readable at thumbnail size.
2. **Picture:** the town at night as a silhouette panorama.
   - Five lit windows, one for each case.
   - The clock tower's window lit too: the mastermind.
3. **The line:** "Five murders. Five killers. One person sent them all."
4. **A banner:** "18,000 SUSPECTS · 5 KILLERS · 1 MASTERMIND".
5. **A strip of names in the corner,** a few of them crossed out, to signal the game instantly.

**Colour:**
- Black silhouette on one bold flat colour.
- Choose it by a thumbnail test: screenshot the amazon.com results for "murder mystery puzzle book", paste the cover thumbnail in, and pick the colour that stands out.

**Size** (142 pages, white paper):
- **Spine:** 142 × 0.002252 = 0.320 in. 142 pages is over KDP's 79-page minimum for spine text. Keep spine text 0.0625 in from the spine edges.
- **Full wrap:** 0.125 + 6 + 0.320 + 6 + 0.125 = **12.57 × 9.25 in**, which is 3,771 × 2,775 px at 300 DPI.
- **Back:** leave a 2 × 1.2 in clear area at the lower right for KDP's barcode.

**Back cover:**
- the blurb (below);
- three bullets;
- a small strip of a real list page;
- one silhouette picture.

## Listing

**Description (draft):**

> **Five murders. Five killers. One person in Juniper Falls sent them all.**
>
> This autumn, five of the town's best-loved people died in five of its best-loved places: the bookstore, the bakery, the diner, the inn and the library. Each time the doors were locked and every visitor's name was written down. Each time, a numbered token was left behind.
>

> - **Illustrated in cut-paper silhouette.** Solutions at the back, upside down.
>
> For fans of cozy mysteries and logic puzzles, and a gift for anyone who reads the last page first (they can't here).

## After publishing: the review QR code

- The last page already carries a neutral review request. It offers nothing and asks for no rating; it asks for an honest review and no spoilers.
- **Paperback ASIN:**
  - It equals the ISBN-10 only for 978 ISBNs.
  - KDP's free ISBNs start with 979, so the ASIN is known only after publishing.
- **Adding the QR code afterwards:**
  1. Set `story.ASIN`, rebuild, and scan the QR code on a phone.
  2. Upload the new interior. The ASIN stays the same.
- The QR code links to `https://www.amazon.com/review/create-review?asin=…`.
