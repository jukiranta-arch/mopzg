# Find-the-killer name lists: where the sweet spot is

Data: amazon.com captures from 2026-09-28/29, plus 67 amazon.co.uk reviews of 12 competitors (see `2026-09-29_whodunit-design-rules.md`). Sales per day are estimated from BSR on one day, so treat them as direction, not exact figures.

Pictures are out (Juho, 2026-09-30): making them takes thousands of AI images, and vector drawings look bad. So the format is crossing off names. The question is how to stay inside what this crowd buys while being different enough to get picked up rather than buried.

**What the pile competes on:** a bigger number of suspects (40,000 → 51,200) and a new setting. Neither gets a book picked up any more. A setting alone flops (Dracula, wizards, dragon egg, cruise, castle), and so does large print.

## 2. What the crowd complains about (67 reviews, 12 books)

The two complaints pull in opposite directions:
- **Too big:**
  - "bogged down in the sheer number of names";
  - "a bit of a grind";
  - letter checks on 20,000 names "would take weeks";
  - *Alice* takes 3–4 weeks.
- **Too easy:**
  - *Never Checked In*: "after 6 clues only 17 left";
  - *Do No Harm*: 45% 1-star, "one clue narrows it to two chapters", done in 20–60 minutes.

Also:
- wrong or ambiguous clues are fatal;
- answers get spoiled by QR codes and websites;
- "just names, no story" (readers want a story that pays off).

Praise goes to:
- famous names hidden in the list ("Easter eggs… a great touch");
- rare finds;
- the answer printed in the book where you can't read it by accident;
- a story.

## 3. The sweet spot

Stay inside the lane on everything the crowd uses to find and buy:
- the cross-out mechanic;
- the "N suspects, M clues, 1 killer" promise in the subtitle;
- the trade-paperback look;
- a solve measured in evenings, not minutes.

Be different on **structure**, which no copycat in the pile has changed.

**Candidate: several linked cases in one book, with a chain that checks itself.**
- **Linked cases:** 4–5 cases of about 4,000–8,000 names each, 25–35k names in total. Each case takes an evening or two, which answers "too big". Each case needs real hunting, which answers "too easy".
- **The chain checks itself:** the killer you name in one case is needed in a clue of the next ("keep only guests who share an initial with the last killer"). If your answer was wrong, the next case falls apart (no suspects, or dozens left), so you know to recheck. That needs no website and spoils nothing. We have not seen this in any competitor.
- **The final case:** all the earlier killers point to one mastermind. That gives a story payoff and a reason to finish.
- **What we keep from the winners:**
  - hidden landmarks to hunt (*Darcy*, *Alice*);
  - page and chapter clues that never clear whole pages on their own;
  - letter clues last, on a few hundred names;
  - clues at the front;
  - the answer at the back, behind a divider.

**Evidence for:**
- *Autumn Killer 3-in-1*: 10/day at 16 days with 1 review.
- *Kill Grid*: 120 connected cases, 74/day, the best newcomer in the category.
- *Phone Book* ("six parts"): 3/day.
- Both main complaints point this way.

**Evidence against:**
- *Layover Murders* (three linked cases) sells 0.1/day. It is also large print, which fails in every book we saw, so the case is not clean.
- *Autumn Killer* sells in a season where every Halloween book is selling.

**Not decided (for the spec):**
- the setting and the victim;
- the pool of landmark names. It must be large and loved (the lesson from Prince Charming), for example real historical people and famous pairs and groups, as *Alice* does;
- the case sizes;
- the price. *Never Checked In* sells 49/day at $6.99; most of the pile is at $13–17.
