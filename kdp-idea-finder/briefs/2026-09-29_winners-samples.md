# What the winning find-the-killer books do

Sources: the Amazon "Read sample" pages of five books, saved by Juho on 2026-09-29, converted to text here with OCR. Samples show the start of each book, never the ending. The samples themselves are not stored in the repo, since they are copyrighted.

| Book | US rank | Rating | Suspects | Sample showed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Killer Isn't Alice | 63 | 4.5★, 1,903 | 46,600 first names | front matter, 40 register pages |
| The Killer Never Checked In | 1,303 | 4.1★, 60 | 3,000 hotel records | story, how to play, map, 10 clue cards, checkpoint A |
| The Killer Was On The Guest List | 9,069 | 4.2★, 159, but 24% 1-star | 48,319 first names + surnames | how to play, all 22 clues |
| Find the Killer (Midnight Murder) | 16,013 | 4.6★, 12 | 10,000 numbered first names | how to play, all 18 clues |
| The Lottery Killer Vol. 2 | UK 4,728 | 82% 5★ | 2,100 portrait cards and 2,300 bids | card pages, back cover |

## How suspects are shown

- **Nobody uses titles.**
  - *Alice* prints bare first names: Luka, Arnold, Lyric. Large, clean type, about 9 names a line and 200–260 a page, no separators, no numbers.
  - *Find the Killer* prints numbered first names in columns.
  - *Guest List* prints first names and surnames, and has to explain "FIRST name, SURNAME or FULL name" in every clue.
  - Only *Who Killed Mr Darcy?* has titles, because its cast is famous screen characters.
- **Records with attributes beat bare names for variety.**
  - *Never Checked In*: entry number, name, arrival day and time, room (floor and wing), reservation code, keycard.
  - *The Lottery Killer*: photo, surname and initial, paddle number, age, country, 16 cards a page.
  - Clues can then be about *what you see* (hat, glasses, a room on the East Wing) as well as letters, which readers love.
- **Chapters are places:** *Alice* has "A Night in Venice", "After Closing Time", "Caernarfon: Part I", "Sequoia Falls", "Aspen, Again". The *Guest List* uses rooms of the house ("The Ballroom"), and one clue is "keep only case files named after a room inside the house".
- **Easter eggs:** *Alice* hides famous groups in the list (the Beatles; the Kardashians; the presidents), and clues use them. A reviewer said: "famous names and characters strategically placed together. A great touch!"

## How clues are written

- **Two layers,** in *Never Checked In* and the *Guest List*:
  - a line of story in a witness's voice ("The minute hand landed on an even number", Mara's note);
  - then a plain rule ("Rule: Keep only even-numbered arrival minutes." / "> Cross out every suspect whose SURNAME begins with a vowel.");
  - *Never Checked In* adds a worked example with rows crossed out, and a "☐ Card #1 complete" tick box.
- **Precise rules pages:**
  - *Find the Killer*: "Vowels are A, E, I, O and U only. Y is never a vowel. Every numbered line is a separate suspect, even when two share the same name… When a clue counts names on a page, count every name printed on that page, including ones you have already crossed out."
  - *Guest List*: navigation labels "Suspect List Page X of 210", so clue references never shift.
- **The mix of clues** (the *Alice* clue list is not in its sample; this is from solvers' published tips):
  - chapter clues ("eliminate chapters without place names", "odd number of pages");
  - landmark hunts ("within 1 page of Sasha", "find Anna and Zack");
  - famous clusters (Beatles, Kardashians);
  - proximity ("within 10 names of a 3-letter name": highlight these);
  - letter clues last, on the highlighted names.
  - Solvers say "some clues save checking thousands of names" and "don't do the clues in order".
- **What goes wrong:**
  - The *Guest List* opens with 9 letter clues on all 48,319 names ("no J or Q", "at least four vowels"…). That is the "comb through 20,000 names" 1-star complaint.
  - *Never Checked In*'s first 6 clues are attribute filters that leave exactly 26 of 3,000, which gets "too easy".
  - *Find the Killer* is all letter clues, which works because the list is only 10,000.

## Checking progress and the answer

| Book | Checkpoints | Hints | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Never Checked In | exact totals, in character: "Twenty-six. If you have more, the ghost has multiplied." Plus what to recheck | 3 levels per clue (what it means, where to look, method) | solution at the back behind a divider: "once you cross it, the mystery cannot be unseen" |
| Find the Killer | "after clue 5 about 902 remain; after clue 9 about 135; after clue 14 about 7" | none seen | solution at the back: "DO NOT FLIP TO THE END" |
| Guest List | none seen | none seen | answer key at the back **in cipher**, "so a glance cannot spoil it" |
| Alice | none in the book | tips and clarifications online | submit online, reply by email |

The common, accepted solution is the simplest one: a solution at the back, behind a clear warning, plus checkpoints.

## What this means for Who Killed Prince Charming?

1. **Drop titles.** Ordinary guests are single first names, as in *Alice* and *Find the Killer*. Fairy-tale characters appear whole ("Snow White", "Old King Cole"). One letter rule: every printed letter counts; Y is a consonant.
2. **Chapters are places in the palace:** the Grand Staircase, the Orangery, the Kitchens, the Rose Garden. Chapter clues can use them ("a room inside the palace, not the grounds").
3. **Fairy-tale clusters as Easter eggs and clues:** the Three Bears in a row, Jack next to Jill, Hansel and Gretel. They make hunting fun.
4. **Clue cards in two layers:** a witness line (the Fairy Godmother, Red Riding Hood, the Mirror…), then a plain rule, then a small worked example. A tick box.
5. **A clue mix like *Alice*:** a few chunk clues that clear pages or chapters (not all at once); hunt and proximity clues that make you scan and highlight; letter clues last, on a few hundred names.
6. **Checkpoints with exact totals** after clue groups, in the witness's voice, with what to recheck.
7. **Answer:** solution at the back behind a "do not read ahead" divider, printed upside down; optional 3-level hints per clue. This replaces the Verdict pages, which no winner uses.
8. **Later, as a series differentiator:** guest cards with attributes (how they arrived: coach, pumpkin, broomstick; which tale they come from), as *Never Checked In* and *The Lottery Killer* do.
