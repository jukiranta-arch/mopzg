## The formula buyers recognise

1. **The title is the picture.**
   - Huge condensed type fills 40–60% of the cover, in 2–4 stacked lines.
   - One word is picked out in an accent colour.
   - It is the only thing readable at thumbnail size, and on every winner it is readable.
2. **A paper "case file" ground:** cream, manila or parchment (6 of 8).
3. **The game is shown.** A list of names with some struck through and one circled (6 of 8). That is how a shopper knows what kind of book it is in one glance.
4. **A silhouette** (5 of 8): a woman, a man with a knife, a hooded figure, a fedora, a head full of names.
5. **The numbers line** in a strip or ribbon: "N suspects · M clues · 1 killer".
6. **Black and red** is the house palette (5 of 8).

**Where the formula leaves room:** the best seller is the one that broke the palette. *Alice* is hot magenta in a row of cream, red and black. It keeps everything else in the formula.

## Where ours can stand out without leaving the formula

- **Colour:** the colours already taken are cream, red and black, magenta (Alice), orange (Autumn), and blue as a frame (Lottery). Free: deep night blue or teal with warm gold, and deep juniper green. A night scene with lit windows gives us a reason for the colour.
- **Picture:** every winner shows a person or a document. **Nobody shows a place.** Our story is a town, so the town is our picture.
- **Silhouette:** familiar to the genre (5 of 8 use one), but ours is hand-cut paper rather than flat vector clip art.
- **Story on the cover:** five lit windows for the five murders, and one more lit window in the clock tower for the mastermind. The cover tells the premise without words.
- **Keep from the formula:**
  - the huge stacked title with one accent word;
  - a list of names with strike-throughs and a circle;
  - the numbers line.
